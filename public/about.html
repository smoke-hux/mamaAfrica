Chapter one

The spice drawer that wouldn't close.

How a Saturday stall turned into a supply chain that starts at the farm gate.

Mama Amina never planned to run a business. She planned to make pilau masala the way her mother did in Mombasa Old Town, whole cumin and cloves toasted the same morning, and to stop paying for a dusty packet that tasted of nothing. Neighbours in Westlands asked for some. Then their cousins. Then a restaurant in Kilimani. Within a year her Saturday stall had become a rented lock-up off Waiyaki Way, and the question changed from "how do I make enough?" to "where does the good stuff actually come from?"

The answer, it turned out, was not the wholesalers at Marikiti. It was a tea pickers' co-op above Kericho, a rice growers' society in the Mwea paddies, a women's coconut group in Lamu. People with names, harvests, and phone numbers that we still have saved.

"If I can't tell you who grew it, I won't sell it to you. That's the whole rule." Amina Salim, founder

Small batches, on purpose

Spices lose their oils within months of grinding. Most of what sits on a supermarket shelf was milled a year ago. We grind monthly in batches of two hundred kilos, cook sauces in eighty-litre sufurias every week, and pack meal kits the morning they're ordered. It costs more. It also tastes like the thing it is supposed to taste like.

Every parcel leaves Westlands the same day with a recipe card written by a home cook from the county the ingredient came from, because the instructions on the back of a packet have never once taught anyone to cook.

What we stand on

Four rules we don't bend.

01

Farmers first

Direct contracts, prices agreed before planting, paid at the farm gate. No brokers, no middlemen.

02

Nothing artificial

No colourings, no anti-caking agents, no "natural flavouring". If it isn't in the recipe, it isn't in the jar.

03

Cook it tonight

Every product comes with a recipe card written by a home cook, tested in a Kilimani bedsit on a two-ring gas cooker.

04

Deliver it fresh

Ground monthly, cooked weekly, packed to order, on a rider's bike the same day. Freshness is the whole point.
