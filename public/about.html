Chapter one

The spice drawer that wouldn't close.

How a Sunday side-hustle turned into a supply chain that starts at the farm gate.

Mama Ngozi never planned to run a business. She planned to make suya spice the way her mother did in Kaduna, coarse groundnut and fierce chilli, and to stop paying six pounds for a dusty jar that tasted of nothing. Neighbours asked for some. Then their cousins. Then a restaurant. Within a year her kitchen drawer of jars had become a rented lock-up, and the question changed from "how do I make enough?" to "where does the good stuff actually come from?"

The answer, it turned out, was not the wholesalers in the industrial estates. It was a co-op of forty groundnut farmers outside Kano, a pepper grower's collective in Tamale, a women's hibiscus association in Kaolack. People with names, harvests, and phone numbers that we still have saved.

"If I can't tell you who grew it, I won't sell it to you. That's the whole rule." Ngozi Okafor, founder

Small batches, on purpose

Spices lose their oils within months of grinding. Most of what sits on a supermarket shelf was milled a year ago. We grind monthly in batches of two hundred kilos, cook sauces in eighty-litre pots every week, and assemble meal kits the day they're ordered. It costs more. It also tastes like the thing it is supposed to taste like.

Every parcel ships within 48 hours with a recipe card written by a home cook from the region the ingredient came from, because the instructions on the back of a packet have never once taught anyone to cook.

What we stand on

Four rules we don't bend.

01

Farmers first

Direct contracts, prices agreed before planting, paid at the farm gate. No auction floor, no middlemen.

02

Nothing artificial

No colourings, no anti-caking agents, no "natural flavouring". If it isn't in the recipe, it isn't in the jar.

03

Cook it tonight

Every product ships with a recipe card written by a home cook, tested in a small flat with a two-ring hob.

04

Ship it fresh

Ground monthly, cooked weekly, packed to order, out the door in 48 hours. Freshness is the whole point.
